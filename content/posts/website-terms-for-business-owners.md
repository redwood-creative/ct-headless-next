When you first decide to build a website, you’ll likely encounter a lot of new terms that might feel a bit overwhelming. But don’t worry—I’m here to walk you through the most common website terms for business owners.

Save this page if you think it might be useful to revisit! Even if you’re planning to hire a web developer, understanding these terms will help you make informed decisions throughout the process.

## Development Website Terms

### **Backend vs. Frontend**

![Infographic contrasting frontend versus backend roles. Backend: server-side logic, security, and data. Frontend: user experience and visuals. Both ensure website functionality—essential website terms for small business owners to understand.](/blog-media/2024/11/Backend-vs-Frontend-1.png)

The backend is everything that happens behind the scenes of a website. Think of it as the “organs” of a body—handling data, server connections, and other foundational functions.

The frontend, on the other hand, is the “skin” and “features” you interact with: the buttons, images, and text. Without the backend, the frontend would be a blank canvas with no content or functionality.

### **Domain & Hosting**

A domain is your website’s unique address (e.g., [*www.clicktailors.com*](/)), similar to a street address for a business. It helps people find your website online.

Hosting is the service that stores your website’s files and makes them accessible to anyone on the internet. You could develop a site on your computer, but without hosting, no one else would be able to view it.

### **CMS (Content Management System)**

A CMS, like WordPress, is a tool that allows you to manage and update website content without touching code. For example, if you want to publish blog posts, a CMS provides a user-friendly space for writing and posting them.

Many CMSs also let you update other elements, like text, images, and basic styling, without diving into code.

### **SSL Certificate**

![ A browser warning screen displays "Your connection is not private," cautioning that potential attackers could steal your information. It suggests returning to safety and highlights the importance of an SSL certificate, a crucial website term for small business owners.](/blog-media/2024/11/image.png)

SSL is the Secure Sockets Layer.

Okay, well that might not help you understand it any better. Let’s try again.

An SSL certificate encrypts data and secures the connection between a website and a visitor’s browser. Sites with SSL display a “lock” icon in the browser’s address bar, signaling that users can trust the connection. Without SSL, visitors might see a “Your Connection is Not Private” warning, which can quickly drive them away.

### **SEO (Search Engine Optimization)**

SEO helps your site stand out among millions of others when people search for keywords. Search engines like Google rely on SEO to rank websites based on factors like relevance, quality, and structure.

Good SEO practices drive organic (non-paid) traffic to your site. It’s a specialized skill, and some agencies focus solely on SEO for businesses.

### **API (Application Programming Interface)**

An API is a set of tools developers use to connect a website to other services. For instance, if you want to add Google Maps to your site, Google’s API enables this by providing the data and code needed to integrate their map. APIs are commonly used to embed third-party services, and some businesses even monetize their own APIs.

### **URL (Uniform Resource Locator)**

![URL in the address bar displaying "clicktailors.com/blog/posts/website-terms-for-business-owners," indicating a blog post that provides essential website terminology for business owners in the context of web development.](/blog-media/2024/11/Screenshot-2024-11-04-at-5.20.33-AM.png)

A URL is a unique address for each web page. Think of it as navigating within an apartment building: the domain (e.g., *[clicktailors.com](http://clicktailors.com)*) takes you to the building, while the specific URL directs you to a particular “apartment” or page.

### **Database**

A database is where a website stores structured information, like user profiles or product listings, for easy access and organization. For example, an e-commerce site may have a “Customer” table that contains each customer’s name, address, and contact details.

### **JavaScript, CSS, and HTML**

HTML (Hypertext Markup Language) is the blueprint of your website’s structure.

CSS (Cascading Style Sheets) dictates how the site looks, controlling colors, fonts, layout, and minor animations.

JavaScript adds interactivity: clicking buttons, filling out forms, and other dynamic features rely on JavaScript to make the frontend “come to life.”

## Design Website Terms

### **User Experience (UX)**

UX is the overall experience a visitor has on your site. Modern web design emphasizes user-friendly UX, as it affects both visitor satisfaction and search rankings. A well-designed UX makes your site easy and intuitive to use, keeping visitors engaged and minimizing confusion.

### **User Interface (UI)**

UI is the look and feel of the website’s interactive elements, like buttons, forms, and menus. Good UI design focuses on making interactions intuitive and visually appealing. Together, UI and UX ensure a website is both functional and enjoyable to use.

### **Wireframe**

A wireframe is a basic blueprint for a site layout. Designers often create wireframes to outline where key elements will go before moving into detailed design work.

### **Typography**

![Typography design featuring the word "TYPOGRAPHY" prominently displayed in large, elegant font, with the words "SIZE," "READABILITY," and "FEELING" listed below, separated by vertical lines, all set against a dark background to emphasize web design principles.](/blog-media/2024/11/Typography.png)

Typography goes beyond font choice; it encompasses how text is arranged, including sizes, readability, and the overall feel. Typography contributes heavily to your brand’s personality.

### **Color Scheme**

A website’s color scheme influences how users perceive your brand. The colors you choose can evoke trust, urgency, or calmness. For example, banks often use blues and greens to create a sense of security.

### **Call to Action (CTA)**

A CTA encourages users to take specific actions, like “Sign Up” or “Contact Us.” Strategic CTAs can drive conversions and guide visitors toward valuable interactions.

### **Accessibility**

Accessibility ensures your website is usable for everyone, including people with disabilities. Adding descriptive “alt text” to images, for example, allows screen readers to convey what’s in an image to visually impaired users.

### **Visual Hierarchy**

Visual hierarchy arranges elements to guide users’ attention to the most important parts of a page. This is achieved through the size of text, color contrasts, and positioning, ensuring that visitors can quickly find the information they need.

### **Branding Elements**

Branding elements, such as logos, fonts, and consistent color schemes, communicate your brand’s identity. A well-executed brand design makes your website memorable.

### **White Space**

White space (or empty space) between elements helps create a clean, readable design. It gives content room to breathe and allows users to focus on the most important parts of the page.

### **Responsive Design**

Responsive design ensures your website looks great on any device, adapting the layout for screens of all sizes. Given the wide variety of devices today, responsive design is vital for providing a seamless experience.
